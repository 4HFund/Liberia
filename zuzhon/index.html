Chapter One · The journey

The road ended.
The story didn't.

To reach Zuzhon, we left the familiar roads behind and continued on foot through the forest. What waited at the end was far more important than the journey itself.

Follow the story ↓
01 / THE WALK

One step
at a time.

After the drive toward Zuzhon, the road gave way to a narrow footpath. Following our Liberian guides, we carried cameras and supplies through the forest, past towering palms and into open stretches of countryside. Every step brought us closer to the school and the people waiting there.

ON THE TRAIL · ORIGINAL FOOTAGE

Walk the path with us.

Follow the narrow forest trail our group traveled with local guides on the way to the village.

A MOMENT ALONG THE WAY

We came to listen and learn.

We were still on the journey, but the people guiding us were already sharing something important: a place, a community, and a story worth taking the time to understand.

02 / REACHING ZUZHON

A school beyond
the forest.

At the end of the walk, we reached the church where children currently attend classes. The community has started constructing a separate school, but it remains unfinished. For now, the church serves as both a place of worship and a place of learning.

03 / THE WELCOME

Then the children
began to sing.

One of the moments we most wanted to preserve was the welcome the children gave us. Their voices tell this part of the story better than any narration could. Turn on the sound and listen.

THE WELCOME · IN PHOTOGRAPHS

Faces we won't forget.

Beyond the singing, we spent time getting to know children and families in Zuzhon. These photographs preserve two of those moments: the children gathered with members of our visiting team. They remind us that the relationships matter more than any single photograph.

ZUZHON · THE UNFINISHED SCHOOL

A school still being built.

The community has begun building a dedicated school, but construction is not yet complete. For now, classes meet in the church building, which serves both as a place of worship and a school. This original footage shows the unfinished school project and the community’s vision for its children.

The new school building remains unfinished. The church currently provides space for both worship and classes.

04 / INSIDE THE SCHOOL
The people and their stories

Inside the classroom,
we listened.

We spent time with students, teachers, and community members in the church building that currently doubles as their school. We saw children at their desks, listened to local leaders, and learned how education and worship share the same space while the dedicated school remains unfinished.

A COMMUNITY AT WORK

These classrooms are more than a backdrop for our trip. The children, educators, and leaders who use them are at the center of the story.

Inside the classroom · Zuzhon school visit
INSIDE THE SCHOOL · PHOTOGRAPHS

A classroom full of stories.

Inside the brick-walled school, we saw students gathered around wooden desks, a chalkboard with lessons written across it, and maps used for learning. We listened, observed, and spent time with the people whose daily work gives this building its purpose.

THE SCHOOL VISIT

Beyond the classroom walls.

Our visit did not end at the classroom door. Outside, children walked alongside our group as we spent time with the community. These are the ordinary, personal moments that made Zuzhon unforgettable.

Walking together outside the Zuzhon school
THE COMMUNITY · BEYOND THE CLASSROOM

Zuzhon is more than a project site.

As we moved through the village, we saw places where families gather, people who welcomed us, and the school building that serves the community. Before talking about what could be developed on the land, we want to show the life and relationships already here.

05 / THE LAND AND THE VISION

What we saw.
What might grow.

Our visit also brought us face to face with land and existing unfinished structures surrounded by vegetation. These photographs show the site as it was during our visit—not a completed agricultural project. The proposed 100-acre cacao, farming, and animal-development vision is still being explored with Liberian leadership and partners.
