The Who and the Why

Not the heroes. The bridge.

The most important voices in this documentary already belong to Liberia. Pastor Bobby and Sidney are going to listen carefully, build trust, and create the conditions for pastors, families, workers, and community leaders to tell their own story with dignity and clarity.

“We are not traveling to become the center of the story. We are going to help the story travel farther.”
Pastor · Listener · Interviewer

Pastor Bobby Westfall

Pastor Bobby will lead relational engagement and interviews, creating space for Liberian church leaders to speak from experience, conviction, and hope. His role is to listen before asking, understand before explaining, and serve the partnership with humility.

Army Veteran · Filmmaker · Storyteller

Sidney Mozingo

Sidney will lead cinematography, photography, audio, and post-production. He approaches this mission with the support of his family and a commitment to build a film with context and excellence—one that protects dignity, centers Liberian voices, and gives churches and partners meaningful media for years to come.

The Visual Journey

From West Virginia to a story shared with the world.

Each step is designed around listening, relationship, documentation, and bringing the story home responsibly.

01The Sending

West Virginia

The Foundery Church sends Pastor Bobby and Sidney with prayer, preparation, and a commitment to represent the mission with care.

02Listening First

Monrovia

Arrival begins with relationships and orientation. Local partners help shape the story before the cameras determine the direction.

03Faith in Community

Local Churches

Worship, preaching, families, pastors, and everyday ministry reveal the human center of the documentary.

04The Vision in the Ground

100-Acre Development

Cacao, animals, food production, training, and employment come together in a long-term vision for sustainable ministry and opportunity.

05The Story Comes Home

Documentary Premiere

The finished film, photographs, interviews, and shorter media return home to build awareness, prayer, partnership, and future support.

Trust and Transparency

Clarity is part of the mission.

Global partnership requires responsible stewardship, realistic planning, and respect for the people whose lives and ministry will appear on screen.

Church-administered givingDonations are processed and administered through The Foundery Church.

Where will the money go?

Funds support approved mission and documentary expenses such as international airfare, in-country transportation, lodging, meals, travel requirements, insurance, media storage, and production needs. Gifts are administered through The Foundery Church.

How is travel safety being handled?

The team will follow the guidance of host-church leaders, current travel requirements, insurance requirements, and real-time local conditions. Plans may be adjusted whenever church leadership or local partners determine that a change is necessary.

Does sponsoring an acre mean I own land?

No. The $60 acre sponsorship is symbolic. It connects a gift to the scale of the 100-acre vision but does not create ownership, equity, or any legal interest in property in Liberia.

Whose story will the documentary center?

Liberian pastors, church leaders, families, workers, and community members will remain at the center. Pastor Bobby and Sidney are traveling as listeners and storytellers, not as the heroes of the film.

What will be created after the trip?

The primary deliverable is a modern documentary, along with photographs, interviews, presentation materials, and short-form media that can support awareness, prayer, partnership, and future fundraising.
